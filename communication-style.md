# Communication Style

This file defines the writing and communication standard for Learn • Think • Verify • Adapt learning content.

It is a domain-specific rule under `REPOSITORY_STANDARD.md` and is used by `CURRICULUM_WORKFLOW.md`. It defines how approved learning content should be written; it does not define repository governance or the curriculum approval workflow.

## 1. Section length

Keep each content section focused and to the point. Do not normally exceed two to three paragraphs within one section.

Each paragraph should contribute directly to the idea being developed. Do not add extra paragraphs merely to make the content appear more complete.

## 2. Writing style

Always write in simple, clear English that is easy to understand without reducing the depth of the ideas. Maintain a professional, human-centred tone throughout the writing. Write with clarity, purpose, and respect for the reader. Every sentence should contribute meaningfully to the idea being developed.

Prefer concise writing over unnecessary complexity. Remove filler words, repetition, and decorative language whenever possible. Simplicity should improve understanding, not reduce intellectual quality.

Never use clickbait, sensationalism, exaggerated claims, or artificial urgency. Avoid unnecessary emojis, internet slang, or influencer-style writing. The goal is to educate, not to impress.

Write like a professional community builder who helps people learn, think, verify, adapt, and grow together.

## 3. Communication style

Always communicate from a shared perspective. Use `we`, `our`, and `us` whenever appropriate to reinforce that Learn • Think • Verify • Adapt is a learning community rather than a one-way teaching platform.

Never position one person as the teacher and everyone else as students. We are all learners. We learn together, think together, verify together, adapt together, improve together, and grow together.

Maintain an encouraging and collaborative voice without becoming overly emotional or motivational. Build trust through honesty, clarity, and thoughtful guidance.

## 4. Paragraph rhythm

Learn • Think • Verify • Adapt values depth over dramatic formatting. Develop ideas through well-structured paragraphs, typically around three to six sentences, while respecting the two-to-three-paragraph limit for each section.

Allow each paragraph to explore one meaningful idea before moving naturally to the next. Avoid excessive one-line or two-line paragraphs because frequent short paragraphs create unnecessary visual drama and interrupt the reader's flow of thought.

Use a single-line paragraph only when introducing a genuinely significant insight, turning point, reflection question, or sentence that deserves deliberate emphasis. Such moments should remain rare so they retain their impact.

Write with a natural conversational rhythm that resembles a thoughtfully written premium book rather than a collection of social-media posts or modern self-help content. The reader should feel guided through a sequence of ideas, not rushed through isolated statements.

The reader should remember the quality of the thinking, not the formatting of the page.

## 5. Human capability remains central

Technology, including AI, may provide context, examples, evidence, or tools. It must not automatically become the permanent centre of the learning content.

When technology changes, explain what the development means for how we learn, think, verify, decide, practise, or adapt. Prefer durable human meaning over temporary product-specific instruction unless the approved curriculum explicitly requires a product-specific example.

## 6. Evidence and claims

Do not strengthen a source claim beyond what the available evidence supports. Separate what the Weekly Intelligence establishes from the human meaning we draw from it.

Do not turn a limited observation into a universal claim. When evidence is uncertain, incomplete, early, or context-dependent, communicate that honestly rather than making the writing sound more certain.

## 7. Morning and evening relationship

Morning content develops the day's learning through understanding, practice, and a clear connection to what comes next.

Evening content should not merely paraphrase the morning. It should help us look back, think together about the meaning of the idea, and move forward with a useful reflection or connection.

## 8. Formatting relationship

This file governs writing style, not the exact WhatsApp layout. The authoritative daily structure, spacing expectations, asterisk treatment, day markers, approval gates, and append-and-freeze process are defined in `CURRICULUM_WORKFLOW.md`.

Do not change the delivery format in the name of writing style. Content quality and formatting consistency are separate requirements, and both must be satisfied.

## 9. Identity

The permanent community identity is:

`Learn • Think • Verify • Adapt`

Do not use an older project or channel name as the community identity in newly generated curriculum content. Technology and AI remain context or tools; the four pillars remain the stable identity.
